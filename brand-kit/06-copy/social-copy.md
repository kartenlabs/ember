## LinkedIn product post

We made Ember, a quiet pomodoro timer with an optional ambient soundtrack for work.

It has adjustable focus and break lengths, five selectable chimes, Night and Daylight themes, and a session log stored in the browser. No account is needed.

The current ambient station is Claude FM — music for thinking and building — published by Claude and embedded through YouTube. It is one part of the working atmosphere; Ember remains an independent kartenlabs project.

The interface is deliberately small. The engineering underneath handles reload recovery, settings changes during a session, and the local calendar boundaries that a session log depends on.

Explore it: https://ember.kartenlabs.com
More from kartenlabs: https://kartenlabs.com

## LinkedIn case-study / document post

What does it take to make a small timer feel considered?

For Ember, the work spanned both the visible interface and the quieter details: mode colors with meaning, restrained typography, mobile layouts, accessible controls, and a clock that recovers its state when a page is reopened.

The ambient station adds an optional soundtrack to the session. It currently features Claude FM by Claude through YouTube, with the original source credited.

## Avoid in publication

Do not say “official Claude timer,” “powered by Claude AI,” “exclusive Claude audio,” or “proven to improve concentration.” Do not claim the entire app makes no third-party requests: the optional YouTube embed is an external service. Session settings and history being local is the accurate statement.
